# Arm N_d closes the physical number ledger, and gives up the operator's

Owner freeze-lift, 2026-08-22. Arm N gives the interface number transfer the
MOIST air-mass ratio and closes the ledger the kernel integrates, because
`dend(i,k) = den(i,k)` (F:870). The physical column number is taken in DRY mass
(`G33-BASIS-006`), and `FINDING_moisture_gradient_basis_v1` measured what Arm N
leaves against it. Arm N_d is the arm that removes it.

## The change

Generated by `make_factorial_variants.py` from `module_mp_kdm6_nmass.F`, two
lines, never hand-edited. With `rho_d = rho/(1+q)` the weight becomes

    rho_d(k+1) dz(k+1)     den(k+1) dz(k+1) (1+q(k))
    ------------------  =  --------------------------
    rho_d(k)   dz(k)       den(k)   dz(k)   (1+q(k+1))

`q(ims:ime,kms:kme)` is in `kdm62D`'s own argument list, so it is in scope on
the line being edited. No new state, no new argument, no reordering.

The generator's length guard refused the first version: the N form is already
106 characters against a canonical maximum of 119, and the two moisture factors
took it to 132. The replacement continues the line instead of lengthening it --
which is what that guard is for, and it fired before anything was built.

## What it does

`g33_fixture_moisture_gradient_v1`, first call, `nr`, residual over starting
inventory, from the ACTUAL `XFER` records:

| arm | moist ledger | dry ledger | dry, as % of legacy's |
|---|---|---|---|
| `legacy` | 3.849e-03 | 3.788e-03 | 100 % |
| `nmass` | 5.775e-08 | -6.583e-05 | 1.74 % |
| **`nmass_dry`** | **6.603e-05** | **-8.209e-09** | **0.0002 %** |

Columns 2 and 3 give 0.0000 % and 0.0003 %. **The acceptance criterion in the
freeze-lift request is met**: `|R_dry|` is at roundoff, not merely smaller.

**And it is a trade, though not between equals.** Arm N_d moves the MOIST
residual from 5.8e-08 to 6.6e-05 -- about the size of the dry residual Arm N
left. That control was written into the request precisely so it could not be
quietly omitted here.

But the two ledgers are not two equally valid choices. `nr` is a mixing ratio
per kg of DRY air (`G33-BASIS-006`), so the physical column number has dry air
as its carrier mass and the moist ledger is not a conservation law -- it is the
measure the legacy operator happens to integrate. The accurate sentence is that
Arm N_d recovers the physical invariant and gives up numerical compatibility
with the legacy moist diagnostic, and which of those a release should prefer is
`G33-BASIS-002`, an owner decision.

## Against the ledger that does not move

The table above measures the dry ledger with THIS call's `qv`, and that is a
moving measure. Dry air does not leave the column during microphysics, so a
denominator that changes with the vapour is not a conserved quantity being
tracked -- it is a quantity being redefined. The repository already holds the
right one: `window_cell_mass(stream, "physical")` takes `qv` from
`G33R INITIAL` and freezes `rho*dz` together, and its docstring records that an
earlier version of THAT function made exactly this mistake. This finding made
it again, in the analyzer and in the arm.

Re-measured against the window-initial physical ledger, residual over surface
flux, whole 12-call window:

| arm | column 1 | column 2 | column 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `legacy` | 1.043e-02 | 1.053e-02 | 7.330e-02 |
| `nmass` | -1.817e-04 | -2.250e-04 | -1.856e-03 |
| **`nmass_dry`** | **2.882e-07** | **-9.812e-08** | **6.641e-04** |

Two things follow, and neither is the sentence this finding first wrote.

**The first-call result stands unchanged.** Re-running it against the
window-initial measure gives -8.209e-09, 9.207e-10 and 9.095e-09 -- the same
numbers, because on the first call the pre-sed `qv` IS the window's initial
`qv`. The moving-measure objection does not bite there.

**Over the window it bites in one column of three.** Columns 1 and 2 close to
3e-07 and 1e-07 -- against Arm N's 1.8e-04 and 2.2e-04, some six hundred and
two thousand times better. Column 3 leaves 6.641e-04, which is 0.9 % of the
legacy defect there while the other two are at 0.003 %.

**Why column 3, answered.** This finding first said the candidate was refuted,
because `qv` moves by 99.9 %, 99.9 % and 99.5 % of its initial value in the
three columns and the excursion therefore does not distinguish them. That
measured the wrong quantity. Arm N_d weights by the CURRENT dry mass while the
ledger is taken in the window-initial one, so what a transfer leaves is not the
size of the moisture excursion but the mismatch between the two ratios,

    eps(u->l, t) = (1+qv_u^0)(1+qv_l(t)) / [ (1+qv_l^0)(1+qv_u(t)) ] - 1

weighted by the transfer that actually crossed. Summed over every call,
sub-step and interface:

| column | predicted | measured | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.000e+00 | 2.781e-01 | -- |
| 2 | 0.000e+00 | -9.596e-02 | -- |
| **3** | **5.067e+02** | **5.059e+02** | **1.0017** |

Column 3 is predicted to 0.17 %. Columns 1 and 2 predict exactly zero and
measure 0.28 and -0.10, which against their surface flux is 2.9e-07 and
-9.8e-08 -- the roundoff they were already reported as closing to.

So the remainder is not unexplained and it is not a property of the arm's
algebra: it is the cost of weighting by a measure that moves while the ledger
does not. An arm that froze the dry mass at call entry would drive it to the
same roundoff the other two columns already sit at, and that is the arm the
next experiment should build.

## The uniform-moisture control, and what it revealed

The request said Arm N_d and Arm N must agree to the last digit on
`g33_fixture_boundary_mapping_v1`, where `qv` is uniform down each column and
the moisture factor is algebraically 1. Compared over the WINDOW-FINAL state
they do not: up to 2.9e-03 relative in ice number.

Measured per call, they are **bit-identical on call 1** and diverge from call 2
onward. The reason is in the fixture, not the arm:

| call | max in-column `qv` spread at the sedimentation site |
|---|---|
| 1 | **0.000e+00** |
| 2 | 9.992e-01 |
| 6 | 9.996e-01 |
| 12 | 9.997e-01 |

**The fixture's uniform moisture survives exactly one call.** After it, the
call's own microphysics has redistributed vapour vertically, so the moisture
factor is a genuine correction and the two arms legitimately differ.

So the control passes in its meaningful form and my statement of it was
under-specified: it named a fixture and not a SPAN. That matters beyond this
arm. Every "uniform `qv`" statement in this campaign -- including the archive
survey in `FINDING_number_basis_gap_v1` that found in-column spread exactly 0.0
across `armn`, `f64` and `migrate` -- was measured at the FIRST call's pre-sed
stage. The scope was right and was never stated.

A cap branch flip was the first hypothesis for the divergence and is REFUTED:
the CAPIN binding pattern is identical across the two arms, 18 interfaces, 0
flips.

## What it does not establish

One fixture, first call, `mstep = 1`, f32. Not a forecast impact, and not a
recommendation: `FINDING_number_basis_gap_v1` bounds what closing the physical
ledger is worth on a real atmosphere, and `FINDING_real_column_batch_v1` gives
the distribution over real columns. Whether that is worth a kernel variant is a
release decision the owner has not been asked for.
